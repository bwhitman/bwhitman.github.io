We're launching new music hardware today! Meet the [AMYboard, a 10HP modular sized synthesizer](https://amyboard.com) with included ins and outs for digital and analog audio, MIDI, and 2 CV channels. Our AMYboard firwmare runs Micropython and can be controlled and programmed over the web, where you can share your own creations with others. But you can also use AMYboard in Arduino as the starting point for your own synths. It's only US$29.90 - a very inexpensive way to get going making your own modular capable synths. It can be powered over USB or a 10-pin modular power cable and comes with a 10HP laser-cut faceplate. [You can buy one today!](https://amyboard.com)

AMYboard is powered by [AMY](https://github.com/shorepine/amy), the synthesis and effects toolkit [DAn Ellis](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=1H4HuCkAAAAJ&hl=en) and I have been working on for the past few years. AMY is an excellent sounding and efficient re-creation of classic analog synths like the Juno-6 and FM synths like the DX-7. It can also be a sampler, play WAV files, or let you make your own synthesizer setups in code from bare oscillators, effects and filters. AMY is open source and works on all sorts of hardware. It already powers some neat synths like the [Diapsonix](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DMDRZ1dwdG4), the [Spark](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VHB0kLcmQHg), and our own [Tulip Creative Computer](https://tulip.computer). The AMYboard is our "reference hardware" for AMY - running on the same ESP32-S3 with 8MB of RAM as Tulip, but with new features for S/PDIF digital audio and audio input support for live effects, sampling and filters. We've also added an SD card for sample storage, and an [I2C host port for connecting your own displays and knobs!](https://github.com/shorepine/tulipcc/blob/main/docs/amyboard/accessories.md)

AMYboard launches with our new [AMYboard online](https://amyboard.com/editor) - a web based simulator and control surface for the AMYboard. You can use AMYboard online before even buying an AMYboard. AMYboard online runs the same core engine as a hardware AMYboard, and you can set up and interact with the synthesizer over the web. When you're ready, you can plug in your AMYboard to your computer and control it in real time over the web, including the capability to edit and send code to your hardware that will boot up the next time you turn it on. AMYboard's default firmware allows anyone to code their synth creations in Python running on the hardware. The AMY synthesis engine runs in bare metal C, but you can load your own "sketches" onto the hardware to build complex environments. For example, [here's a generative house track](https://www.amyboard.com/editor/?mode=simulate&env=house_generator&user=shorepine&tab=code) with a Derrick May custom "WOODPIANO" FM patch. Or [here's a TB303 + 808 acid setup](https://www.amyboard.com/editor/?mode=simulate&env=acid_generator&user=shorepine&tab=patch). Or [add a filter and reverb to a DX7 patch](https://www.amyboard.com/editor/?mode=simulate&env=dx7filt&user=shorepine&tab=patch). AMYboard online also comes with AMYboard World, a way for the community to share sketches with each other! Please share your own when you make something cool.

AMYboard's low price is its best feature. DAn and I worked for the past year with our friends at [Makerfabs](https://www.makerfabs.com/amyboard.html?srsltid=AfmBOor14R-ylsnMulcmIBZ_YBOLgElKZIQ-6Vn1W_TJmIwVHaKJ5OJD) to get the AMYboard down in price as low as possible, but still with a great quality audio DAC and ADC, digital audio and quality 3.5mm jacks. We want the AMYboard to be a canvas for anyone to make the synth of their dreams, and not have to spend the often 10 times (!) minimum entry fee that most other modular type synthesizers charge to get there. It's clearly not a flashy designer product - you'll want to make your own front panel labels or bring your own knobs and screens -- but the stuff that matters is top rate and a great starting point for your ideas. We want to see many of you helping us along with features, shared sketches and future ideas. Everything about AMYboard is open source. If you've got a [Tulip](https://tulip.computer), you know that we've stlil been adding new funcitonality two years later. 

Here's some things you can do with your $29.90 AMYboard. Please head on over to the [AMYboard website](https://amyboard.com) and pick one up -- or five! (you can easily chain AMYboards together via S/PDIF and I2C.) Find us on our [Discord](https://discord.gg/TzBFkUb8pG) or [Github](https://github.com/shorepine/tulipcc/blob/main/docs/amyboard/README.md) for more!

 - Play accurate Juno-6 and DX7 patches (and make your own) from MIDI or CV input
 - Map CV to MIDI and vice versa
 - Sample live audio and play it back with pitch and filter control
 - Trigger audio samples from an SD card
 - Compose generative or reactive music in Python
 - Build your own synthesis engine in Arduino
 - Swap the analog ports from 1vpp (line) to 10vpp (modular) 
 - Add an OLED screen and a rotary encoder 
 - Use as a USB MIDI interface on your computer
 - Set MIDI CCs or CV input scale to control synth parameters
 - Sonify audio from the internet over Wi-Fi
